    Is this worth the money?
    The case for it, including the parts against us.




                                     What is a robotics engineering programme, and does it
                                     really help my child?
                                     It is a robotics course built around real hardware rather than a screen. Your child
                                     works through 50+ guided builds and finishes three capstone robots they keep.
                                     Most robotics classes hand over a kit and a video. Ours assigns a Teaching Assistant,
                                     so when a servo jams at 9pm the child asks them, not you.

                                     It helps in three ways:

                                         It Builds Judgement: They decide what to make, what to charge and what to cut.
                                         Nobody marks those decisions. They find out when a buyer says no.


                                         It Builds Follow-Through: Interest runs out around week nine. There are months
                                         after that, with a deadline and three teammates waiting. Getting past it is most of
                                         what they learn.


                                         It Builds a Record: Three finished robots, 50+ builds behind them, and video of
                                         each one working. A report card shows none of that.


                                     It will not raise a maths grade. If your child needs marks this year, coaching is the better
                                     spend.




                               How to tell a good robotics
                               programme from an expensive one
                               Six things worth checking, whoever you are speaking to. One of them we would fail.

                               1. Ask to see the full curriculum before you pay. If it is only available after a phone call, there is a
                               reason. Ours is on this site.
                               2. Ask how many students are in a batch. Above twelve, your child will not be known by name.
                               We cap at eight.
                               3. Ask what happens when something breaks. A programme that charges for replacement parts is
                               selling you a kit.
                               4. Ask who is actually in the room. Not the founder's credentials. The person who will sit with your
                               child on a Saturday.
                               5. Ask what happens if your child wants to stop. Look for a refund window and a way to pay in
                               parts. If the whole year is due upfront and non-refundable, that tells you what they expect.
                               6. Ask how long they have run and how many finish. We would fail this one. Four cohorts. Our
                               completion numbers are too new to mean much. Ask us again in two years.




                               CBSE teaches computational thinking and AI from Grade 3. For Grades 6 to 8 it is marked
                               by project, not exam. Skill Subject 417 covers it at Grades 9 and 10. So the work your child
                               does here can go towards school submissions.

                               Most Grade 9 students can explain how a sensor works. Very few have wired one up and
                               had it not work. Finding the fault and fixing it takes time a school timetable does not have.
                               That gap is what this programme is for.

                               Software is getting better at writing code. It is not getting better at spotting a problem
                               worth solving, or at building a thing that works.
