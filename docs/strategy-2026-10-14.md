# Direction rethink — 14 Oct 2026 (development paused here)

## Where we are
Draft 3 is live. Adarsh: "20% done, right direction. Still not happy with the font. Not happy with the landing page." Nothing is concluded yet; these are his thoughts plus Claude's proposals. Agree on direction before building.

## Adarsh's brief (his words, paraphrased)
- Most of the time we are selling to the **parent**, so the site should be parent-centric first, kid-centric after.
- The parent must feel: *my child will learn something good and do something great in the future, so buy it.*
- Ideas: a **video** of a kid building something (say the Sentry), then showing it to parents, parents delighted. A video of a kid **showcasing a project at school**, friends clapping, "wow". Relatives and parents applauding.
- The kid (13–15) will also explore the site himself. He should **not** feel "this is a very professional website". The **kid feel is missing** right now. When the kid sees it, he should think: *this is real, I have to learn this.*
- Wants photos and videos of kids building, showing off, people applauding.

## Claude's proposals (to discuss)
1. **One page, one story, two readers.** Tell the story through the kid, but put the camera where the parent stands. The key shot in every video is the *parent's face* when it works. Parent reads "my child can do this"; kid reads "it works and everyone's impressed".
2. **Page arc:** hook the parent → hand over to the kid → hand back to the parent to pay.
   Hero film + 5–6 words (parent) → *The moment it works* → *Show it off* (school/relatives clips) → *What they actually build* (machines, build chapter, lab — kid energy, fast, loud, numbers) → *What you'll see at home* → *Is it for your kid* → *Reserve*.
3. **Three kinds of video, not one:**
   - Hero film, 25–30 s, slow, no narration: bench → fail → small fix → dart fires → kid turns → parent's reaction.
   - Show-and-tell clips, 10–15 s: school demo, relatives at home, applause. Used as proof with one-line captions.
   - Raw lab cuts, 3–6 s each: macro board, servo twitch, slow-mo dart, terminal code, LED matrix. For the kid half; loop silently.
   - Must be real kids/families (staged actors get spotted). Shoot with the existing cohort if one exists; parental consent for minors on camera.
4. **"Cool / this is real" for a 14-year-old** comes from realness, not cartoon: hardware macro shots, numbers (RPM, mm, g, V), hazard stripes, "KIT No. 014" numbering, firmware version, LIVE badges, code that really runs, dart counter, leaderboard, "Built by Aarav, 14, Indore" stamps. References: Teenage Engineering, Nothing, Bambu Lab, Framework. Hardware is the hero.
5. **Font:** Plus Jakarta Sans reads "safe SaaS". Candidates (free): **Clash Display** (headlines, strongest fit), **Bricolage Grotesque** (youthful, attitude), **Space Grotesk** (techy); body **Satoshi** or **General Sans**. Or license Matter (Fauna's). Proposed next step: a type-test page with the same hero copy in 3–4 pairings side by side.
